# A11 – SLA Snap Fits

## Objective
For this assignment, we are to design modle and print using the SLA printers a functional snap-fit artifact. Our design must identify a working snap fit between two or more components, as well as be creative and show usefulness and not a simple plain test block. 

## Brainstorming and Drawings

For my design I want to make a box that is big enough to enclose all my previous small parts and design from this class to keep the store away as keepsakes and so they don't get lost. They are currently scattered on my desk, and I want them to stay organized and safe from falling off or getting lost. This won't just be a box with a lid that snaps on, though. I want to make two separate snap fits on it. One will be a hinge that will be two different parts, one connected to the lid and one connected to the body of the box. This hinge will have an opening slit on the back side that will hold the other piece in place but will also allow for insertion and the ability to be printed as a separate part. Printing a hinge as one piece isn't possible, to my knowledge, without having to break away material and causing rough surfaces and problems with rotation. The other snap fitment will be hooks that will hang from the lid after attaching the hinge and allow the box to click shut to the bottom piece. They will be cantilever beam-style pieces and will have a slot on the bottom piece where they will snap into place.

After putting it into an assembly to test the fit, I don't like how big the rounded extrusions for the hinge look. Resin and SLA printing is not as flexible as PLA or PETG when using FDM printing and making snap fits, so I wanted to make sure that it was just enough for the rotation to work without having too much extra material, making it hard to snap into place and risk breaking the product. 

<img width="742" height="293" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/659e694b-a00c-40ac-8231-25f0f514b717" />

<img width="538" height="294" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9cc3cd18-8c22-4a93-b1fe-eca9537a3c52" />

So, to fix this, I went in and halved the length of the original extrusion to .10 in, and changed the inner divot on the top piece to .15in deep. I left it slightly bigger so there would be some clearance, and I would have to worry about it being too small after the shrinking from the curing process. 

<img width="428" height="283" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/52affc68-4214-4089-aec9-eda9c5559484" />

After running the design in an assembly and mating the pieces together, I found that the lid actually overlaps onto the body of the box. I have a few ways I could fix this, one being to move the top piece of the hinge down or shrink the lid thickness. 

<img width="887" height="311" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fcd08a16-2c46-4b51-83d5-2aa15553a1d0" />

<img width="419" height="352" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2fcbc6dc-d5f2-4d89-9eea-f9ac5b3cead1" />

I decided to do an extrude cut to remove some of the thickness from the lid instead of changing to the original feature because I didn't want to mess up the hinge geometry and where it sat on the lid, since that was the area where the alignment issues were coming from. So after removing some of the thickness and running another assembly, everything fit correctly with no interference.
