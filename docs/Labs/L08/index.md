# A8 – Design Something Useful

## Objective
The task of this assignment is simply to design something useful for someone or yourself to solve a problem you come up with. We are to document who, what, and how we came by this problem and then how we are going to solve it.

## Reading- Great Questions Lead to Great Design: A Guide to the Design-thinking Process
Why does asking smart questions matter? I think asking smart questions is important because it helps you really understand what you are solving before you attempt to solve it. In engineering, no two problems are going to be the exact same. They may be similar in ways, but to solve every problem, you have to understand more than a formula and numbers. SO asking questions such as why does that happen and what makes it happen, or where do those values come from and how does it change if I do this. This avoids blindly believing every value and assuming what is happening, and you can just repeat that process for every problem. It allows you to develop a way of thinking to create solutions before solving. 


## Brainstorming
This project actually took me a while to brainstorm and think of an issue I need solved. Designing something useful seems so easy, but having that much free rein over what to do can be hard since I'm new to engineering and 3d design. So I just thought: what is something I do every day, and how can I make it easier? I thought about organizing and making things in my life cleaner and easier to navigate, and that's when I thought of my nightstand. Every night I take whatever is in my pockets and just throw it all over my nightstand, scattering it everywhere, and sometimes they may roll or fall off. I carry relatively the same items with me every day, everywhere. A pencil, chapstick, my wallet, car key, AirPods, and my phone. So I decided I am going to make an organization stand completely personalized for what I use in life, with a dedicated slot for each as well as a spot for extra stuff. 

### Measurements
Before I could begin modeling, I needed to know roughly how big to make the object based on how big everything it is holding is. I didn't want everything to be a tight fit; I wanted it to have some wiggly/finger room for me to grab the objects out of and easily slide them into place. With this, I rounded all the measurements up .1-.2in depending on what the object was and how it was shaped.

Next, I made space for my wallet. For this one, I had to extrude upwards to have more height since a wallet is a lot bigger than the rest of the objects. I made a rectangle in the top left corner of the base and dimensioned it to 5 in by 1.5 in and offset it by .2in. This time the offset wasn't for wall thickness; it was just for aesthetics and to keep everything even and not on the edge of the base. I extruded that to a height of .45in. Next, I didn't add another sketch on top of that extrusion of a rectangle with each wall thickness being .1in. I didn't need to dimension the lines, only how thick the walls are to be, since this is an extrude cut.

The next section to make is the AirPods holder. This process was relatively the same as the wallet since the AirPods case is a little bigger than .4in which would be the depth if I went ahead and extrude cut it without building the walls up. This depth may have worked, but I was worried about the case toppling over the top, so I wanted to make it a little taller but still shorter than the overall height because I want it to be loosely sitting in there and have room to grab out.

The next section I made was the extra spaces, as well as the space where my car keys would sit. My car keys aren't a definitive shape since there are multiple pieces that move and jangle around. Making a specific spot for them to slide into would be unnecessary since I have different keys, so being able to throw them all together is good enough for me. I started by loosely sketching a rectangle in the middle of all the other cuts without dimensioning it. This is because I want it to take up as much space as possible. All I did was offset it by 0.2 in from each edge and cut out what was near. This will make it as large as possible while being evenly spaced and organized. I then extrude cut it to .35in.

This dimension was chosen simply by looking at how it came out on the model.

For the slicing and printing process, I wanted to cut as much time as possible since my object is quite large, being 8x5 in. So I set the infill all the way down to 5% since this isn't going to be load-bearing at all, besides a few ounces of the weight of the objects it holds, so it doesn't need to be strong. I also set it to a lightning pattern to save more time. I used PC-05. However, these settings failed twice, and this could have been the machine or the fact that I had the infill too low. 

<img width="959" height="439" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f670e31c-5e44-43a4-9591-2ce2ddb8d274" />

Because of it failing and me having to spend 5 hours on the first two prints, I decided to leave it all original and had a 15% infill with a grid pattern.
